## Significant Development Detected

### Nationwide FAA Ground Stop and Ongoing Air Travel Disruptions Due to Air Traffic Control Telecommunications Instability

- **Time:** Initiated approximately 3:30–3:36 p.m. ET on November 13, 2026; ground stop lifted around 8:15 p.m. ET the same day. Recovery and residual cancellations/delays continued through November 15–16, 2026. Official updates as of November 15–16 confirm system stability and return toward normal operations.
- **Official source:** Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), Department of Homeland Security (DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin), Department of Transportation (Secretary Sean Duffy), White House.
- **Links to original statements:**
  - FAA: [Nationwide Ground Stop: Questions and Answers](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/nationwide-ground-stop-questions-and-answers) (November 15, 2026)
  - DHS: [ICYMI: Secretary Mullin and Secretary Duffy Address Nationwide FAA Ground Stop of All Flights](https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/11/13/icymi-secretary-mullin-and-secretary-duffy-address-nationwide-faa-ground-stop-all) (November 13, 2026)
  - White House: [ICYMI: After Nationwide Ground Stop, Trump Administration Prioritizes Safety, Calls for Full Modernization Funding](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/11/icymi-after-nationwide-ground-stop-trump-administration-prioritizes-safety-calls-for-full-modernization-funding/) and [ICYMI: Secretary Duffy’s Full Sunday Circuit Outlines Ground Stop Response and the Path Forward](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/11/icymi-secretary-duffys-full-sunday-circuit-outlines-ground-stop-response-and-the-path-forward/) (November 15, 2026)
- **Summary:** On November 13, 2026, the FAA issued a rare nationwide ground stop after legacy air traffic control telecommunications equipment (FTI network) experienced instability. Data flooded the network at a rate of approximately 1,000 connection attempts every 4–5 seconds, disrupting communications and radar feeds at 17 of 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers and multiple TRACONs. Preliminary investigation points to a radar site in Amarillo, Texas, and a radio communications site in El Dorado, Arkansas, where contractor L3Harris was performing routine work upgrading from copper to fiber and deploying a new router. Officials repeatedly stated there is zero evidence of a cyberattack or external nefarious actor. Approximately 3,500 aircraft in the air at the time of the stop landed safely. The ground stop lasted roughly 4–5 hours and was lifted once the system stabilized. Thousands of flights were canceled or delayed over the following days (over 12,000 cancellations reported for November 13 alone by some counts; residual impacts continued into November 16). As of November 16, the system is reported stable, it is safe to fly, and operations are returning to normal, with full recovery expected by the end of the day or shortly thereafter. DHS Secretary Mullin characterized the outdated system as a national security risk and stated that the President has declared this a national emergency in the context of the need for Congress to fully fund modernization (an estimated $50 billion request has been referenced; $12.5 billion previously secured). No formal published presidential proclamation specific to a new National Emergencies Act declaration solely for this incident was located in public White House records as of this brief.
- **Area affected:** Nationwide (U.S. National Airspace System; all U.S. airports for departures during the ground stop; residual impacts on domestic and international flights to/from the U.S.).
- **Immediate public action recommended:** None for the general public beyond checking flight status with airlines and remaining patient during recovery. Officials emphasize that it is safe to fly and that all aircraft landed safely. Travelers should monitor airline updates.
- **Confidence level:** Multiple Official Sources (FAA, DHS, DOT, White House statements and Q&A).
