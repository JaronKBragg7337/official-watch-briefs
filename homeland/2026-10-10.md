### Alerts

**1. Federal Emergency Declarations for Hurricane Isaias**
- **Time:** Announced October 9–10, 2026
- **Official source:** FEMA (President Donald J. Trump approvals)
- **Link:** https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20261009/president-donald-j-trump-approves-emergency-declaration-florida (Florida; parallel release for Alabama) and https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20261009/fema-strategically-positions-personnel-and-resources-support-state-and-local
- **Summary:** President Trump approved emergency declarations for Florida and Alabama authorizing FEMA to coordinate federal disaster relief, provide equipment/resources, and support emergency protective measures (75% federal funding for certain actions) in specified counties due to Hurricane Isaias. FEMA activated the National Response Coordination Center and Region 4 Regional Response Coordination Center, deployed Incident Management Assistance Teams, positioned Urban Search and Rescue teams and supplies, and urged residents to finish preparations.
- **Area affected:** Multiple counties in the Florida Panhandle/northern Florida and Alabama (coastal and inland areas in the storm path).
- **Immediate public action recommended:** Yes — Follow local officials, evacuate if ordered, monitor alerts, and consult ready.gov. Residual flooding, wind, and power-outage risks continue after landfall.
- **Confidence level:** Official Statement (Multiple Official Sources)

**2. State Evacuation Orders and States of Emergency (Florida and Alabama)**
- **Time:** Issued primarily October 7–9, 2026; status ongoing as of October 10
- **Official source:** Florida Governor Ron DeSantis / Florida Division of Emergency Management; Alabama Governor Kay Ivey / Alabama Emergency Management Agency
- **Links:** Florida executive orders (e.g., EO 26-202 and amendments) via flgov.com; Alabama via ema.alabama.gov and governor.alabama.gov
- **Summary:** States of emergency were declared covering dozens of counties. Mandatory evacuations applied to non-residents/tourists/visitors in designated coastal zones (Alabama: parts of Mobile and Baldwin counties including Gulf Shores, Orange Beach, Dauphin Island; Florida: various Panhandle zones in counties such as Bay, Escambia, Okaloosa, Santa Rosa, Walton, Franklin, and Gulf, plus low-lying areas/mobile homes). Voluntary orders and local measures were also issued. Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin, Florida, as a Category 2 (105 mph winds) on the evening of October 9 and has since weakened to a post-tropical cyclone; flooding, wind, and power-outage impacts (hundreds of thousands affected) continue inland across the Southeast.
- **Area affected:** Gulf Coast/Florida Panhandle and southern Alabama, with residual impacts extending into Georgia and other Southeast states.
- **Immediate public action recommended:** Yes — Heed remaining local evacuation, shelter, or road-closure guidance; avoid flooded roads; prepare for power outages and residual hazards; follow local EMA updates.
- **Confidence level:** Official Statement (Multiple Official Sources)

No other matching official alerts detected. (A DPRK ballistic missile launch on October 3 was assessed by U.S. Pacific Command as posing no immediate threat to U.S. personnel, territory, or allies. Routine NORAD intercepts in the Alaskan ADIZ are not characterized as requiring public action.)
