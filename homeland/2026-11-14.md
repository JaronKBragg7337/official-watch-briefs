**Homeland Security Brief – November 14, 2026**

**Alert: Nationwide FAA Ground Stop Due to Air Traffic Control Telecommunications Outage (Lifted)**

- **Time:** Issued approximately 3:54–4:00 p.m. ET on November 13, 2026; lifted at 8:15 p.m. ET on November 13, 2026. Ongoing recovery as of November 14 morning.
- **Official source:** Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Secretary Markwayne Mullin, Department of Transportation (DOT) Secretary Sean Duffy, White House briefing.
- **Link to the original statement:** [FAA Statement on Nationwide Ground Stop](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/faa-statement-nationwide-ground-stop); [DHS ICYMI release](https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/11/13/icymi-secretary-mullin-and-secretary-duffy-address-nationwide-faa-ground-stop-all).
- **One paragraph summary:** On the afternoon of November 13, the FAA issued a rare nationwide ground stop for all departing flights at U.S. airports after telecommunications instability affected radar and communications at Air Route Traffic Control Centers and TRACONs. The stop was ordered out of an abundance of caution for safety while more than 3,500 aircraft were airborne; all were accounted for and safely managed under existing procedures, with exceptions for medevac, critical cargo, and law enforcement. Officials attributed the issue to overloaded analog switches and routers in the aging air traffic control system, reported zero evidence of a cyberattack or other nefarious activity at the time, and noted that systems were tested and restored before the stop was lifted. Thousands of flights were canceled or delayed; operations began returning to normal on November 14 with residual disruptions expected.
- **What area is affected:** Nationwide U.S. airspace (all U.S. airports for departures; international arrivals handled case-by-case during the stop).
- **Whether immediate public action is recommended:** No. Officials repeatedly stated the public and travelers were safe, this was not a terrorist or attack event, and the priority is safe recovery. Travelers should check directly with airlines for status, as residual cancellations and delays continue. No shelter-in-place, evacuation, or other public protective actions were issued.
- **Confidence level:** Multiple Official Sources (FAA, DHS, DOT/White House).

No other significant homeland security developments matching the monitored criteria (missile threats, nuclear incidents, major terrorist events, DEFCON changes, widespread civil defense orders, critical infrastructure cyberattacks, or new national emergency proclamations beyond the framing of this outage) were detected in official channels as of this brief. A localized U.S. Coast Guard response to a vessel collision on the Mississippi River near New Orleans was noted but does not meet the nationwide/homeland criteria.
