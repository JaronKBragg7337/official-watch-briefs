## Alert: Hurricane Isaias — evacuation orders and life-threatening storm surge on the central Gulf Coast

- **Time:** National Hurricane Center Advisory Number 12, 10:00 a.m. CDT (15:00 UTC), Friday, October 9, 2026. FEMA preparedness release dated October 8, 2026. State emergency declarations and local evacuation orders issued October 8–9, 2026, and still in effect.
- **Official source:** National Hurricane Center / National Weather Service (NOAA); FEMA; state emergency management (Florida Division of Emergency Management; Alabama governor’s order as reported with local county orders).
- **Link:** https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/mobile/text/MIATCPAT4.html — FEMA: https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20261008/fema-urges-residents-prepare-hurricane-isaias — FEMA preparedness note: https://www.fema.gov/blog/get-ready-hurricane-isaias
- **Summary:** At 10:00 a.m. CDT, Hurricane Isaias was a major hurricane with maximum sustained winds of 120 mph, centered about 195 miles south of Pensacola, Florida, and moving toward the western Florida Panhandle. The National Hurricane Center said the center was expected to reach the western Florida Panhandle coast this evening, with life-threatening storm surge and devastating winds likely along the Florida Panhandle and Alabama coasts. A hurricane warning was in effect from Ocean Springs, Mississippi, to the Bay/Gulf County line in Florida, with a storm surge warning from the mouth of the Mississippi River to the Suwannee River. NHC rainfall guidance called for 4 to 8 inches, locally up to 15 inches, across southern Alabama, the Florida Panhandle and Big Bend, and southwest Georgia through the weekend. FEMA said on October 8 that the National Response Coordination Center and FEMA Region 4’s Regional Response Coordination Center in Atlanta were activated, Incident Management Assistance Teams were deploying to Alabama and Florida, and supplies were being prepositioned at Maxwell Air Force Base in Montgomery. Governors of Florida, Alabama, and Georgia have declared states of emergency. Mandatory local evacuation orders cover portions of Florida’s Walton, Okaloosa, Franklin, and Escambia counties (coastal and low-lying zones, barrier islands, mobile homes, and RV parks, depending on the county). Alabama ordered nonresidents and tourists out of Gulf Shores, Orange Beach, Dauphin Island, and related coastal zones in Baldwin and Mobile counties.
- **Area affected:** Coastal Mississippi (Ocean Springs eastward in the hurricane warning), the Alabama coast, the Florida Panhandle and Big Bend, and inland southern Alabama and southwest Georgia for wind and heavy rain. Not a nationwide event.
- **Immediate public action recommended:** Yes, for people inside ordered evacuation zones, storm-surge warning areas, low-lying flood zones, and mobile homes or RVs in the warning area. NHC and FEMA instruct residents to follow local evacuation and other instructions promptly. People outside ordered zones should monitor local emergency management and NWS forecasts; no nationwide shelter-in-place or civil-defense order is in effect.
- **Confidence:** Official Statement (NHC Advisory 12 and FEMA October 8 release). State and county evacuation details are Official + Independent Reporting (Florida Division of Emergency Management tools and contemporaneous reporting of governor and county orders).
