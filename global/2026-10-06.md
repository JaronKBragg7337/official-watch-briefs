## 1. U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports — 130th vessel redirected

- **Label:** Military Statement
- **Time posted:** 5 October 2026, 4:56 p.m. EDT (20:56 UTC)
- **Source account:** U.S. Central Command (@CENTCOM)
- **Link:** https://x.com/CENTCOM/status/2107213458030641634
- **Country:** United States (operation directed at Iran / Strait of Hormuz approaches)
- **Summary:** CENTCOM said that as of 5 October its forces had redirected 130 commercial vessels while enforcing the ongoing U.S. naval blockade against ships attempting to enter or depart Iranian ports. The command said the blockade was resumed on 14 July at the direction of the commander in chief. In a companion release, CENTCOM said U.S. forces have disabled three ships that refused to comply and, over the past 12 weeks, destroyed 13 commercial vessels that were violating the blockade or tied to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps shadow network. Adm. Brad Cooper, CENTCOM commander, said forces would act against any vessel trying to run the blockade and advised mariners to monitor Notice to Mariners and contact U.S. naval forces on bridge-to-bridge channel 16 in the Gulf of Oman and Strait of Hormuz approaches. This is a blockade-enforcement update, not a new launch announcement.
- **Estimated confidence:** High (direct CENTCOM statement)

## 3. Iranian Army claim of accelerated missile and drone production

- **Label:** Government Claim (military spokesman, via state media)
- **Time posted:** 6 October 2026, reported in Iranian state outlets early on 6 October (Tehran morning; roughly overnight to early morning EDT)
- **Source account:** Iranian Army spokesman Brig. Gen. Mohammad Akraminia, quoted by IRNA / IRIB and reported by Iran International and Gulf News
- **Link:** https://www.iranintl.com/en/202610060989
- **Country:** Iran
- **Summary:** Iranian Army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia said the armed forces accumulated a large stockpile of newly developed offensive and defensive weapons, including missiles and drones, and that production continued daily through what Tehran calls the Ramadan war and is still continuing. Iranian state broadcaster IRIB separately quoted a defense-ministry spokesperson saying weapons and defense-equipment production capacity is about 2.5 times the pre-war level, with some lines more than triple. No inventory figures, ranges, or transfer destinations were given, and the claims have not been confirmed by U.S., Israeli, or IAEA public statements in this window. Independent outlets note assessments that Iran may retain a substantial residual missile and drone force, while much above-ground production infrastructure was damaged earlier in the year.
- **Estimated confidence:** Moderate that the spokesmen made the claims; Low on the unverified production and stockpile figures
