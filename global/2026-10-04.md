## 1. North Korea intermediate-range ballistic missile launch

- **Label:** Confirmed by Multiple Official Sources
- **Time posted:** U.S. Pacific Command statement posted 2026-10-03 00:13 UTC (2026-10-02 20:13 EDT). Launch reported by Seoul and Tokyo early 2026-10-03 KST (about 06:30 KST / 2026-10-02 17:30 EDT). KCNA state-media account of the drill published 2026-10-04 KST (reported by wire services late 2026-10-03 UTC).
- **Source accounts:** U.S. Pacific Command (@USPACOM); Japan Prime Minister’s Office (@JPN_PMO); Republic of Korea Joint Chiefs of Staff (official military detection statement, carried by Korean official/media channels); Korean Central News Agency (DPRK state media, reported by Reuters and Yonhap)
- **Links:** https://x.com/USPACOM/status/2106175757437620629 ; https://www.pacom.mil/Media/News/News-Articles/Article/4618518/us-pacific-command-statement-on-dprk-missile-launch/ ; https://x.com/JPN_PMO/status/2106148201048998113 ; https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/north-korea-says-it-conducted-intermediate-range-strategic-missile-drill-2026-10-03/
- **Country:** North Korea (launch); United States, Japan, and South Korea (detection and response statements)
- **Summary:** Official allied accounts say the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea launched one ballistic missile from the Wonsan area toward the East Sea (Sea of Japan) at about 06:30 KST on 3 October 2026. The ROK Joint Chiefs of Staff assessed the missile flew more than 700 km; Japan’s defense authorities separately cited a track on the order of 680 km and said the missile came down outside Japan’s exclusive economic zone. U.S. Pacific Command said it was consulting allies and that, on current assessments, the launch did not pose an immediate threat to U.S. personnel, U.S. territory, or allies. South Korea’s Office of National Security convened an emergency security review. On 4 October, KCNA reported that Kim Jong Un oversaw a firing drill of an intermediate-range strategic missile tied to a hypersonic strategic weapon system, claiming a 1,000 km impact and framing the shot as development of a nuclear war deterrent. Kim Yo Jong, cited in the same state-media cycle, said South Korea and Japan lost the track after a pull-up on a “wave-like” trajectory. The launch followed a DMZ mine incident that wounded three ROK soldiers and a North Korean rejection of Seoul’s apology demand. Range figures differ between ROK/Japan tracking and the DPRK claim and have not been independently reconciled.
- **Estimated confidence:** High that a ballistic missile was launched and detected by U.S., ROK, and Japanese authorities. Moderate on the DPRK’s hypersonic and 1,000 km claims, which rest on state media.

## 2. Fifteen states, including Iran, China, and Russia, oppose attacks on safeguarded nuclear facilities

- **Label:** Independent Reporting (of a claimed joint government statement)
- **Time posted:** Reported 2026-10-04 (about 09:22 EDT in the circulating write-up). The statement is described as issued Thursday, 1 October 2026, at the 70th IAEA General Conference.
- **Source account:** Not an official social post in this scan. Circulating report attributes the text to Iran, Russia, China, Belarus, Brazil, Burkina Faso, Cuba, Indonesia, Iraq, Nicaragua, Niger, Pakistan, South Africa, Tajikistan, and Zimbabwe.
- **Link:** https://iaea.einnews.com/article/943352512
- **Country:** Iran, China, Russia, and twelve other listed states (IAEA General Conference, Vienna)
- **Summary:** Reporting on 4 October says fifteen countries, including Iran, China, and Russia, issued a joint statement at the IAEA General Conference asserting that peaceful nuclear facilities under IAEA safeguards must not be attacked or threatened, that such action would violate the UN Charter, international law, the IAEA Statute, and the purposes of the NPT, and that attacks could cause radioactive release and damage the safeguards system. The write-up places the statement in the context of prior U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear sites. This scan did not retrieve a matching IAEA Information Circular or a same-day post from the Iranian, Chinese, or Russian foreign ministries confirming the 1 October text, so it is carried as reporting of a government claim rather than a confirmed multi-source official release.
- **Estimated confidence:** Moderate

## 3. IDF strikes on Palestinian Islamic Jihad rocket and anti-tank missile operators in Gaza

- **Label:** Military Statement
- **Time posted:** 2026-10-03 13:09 UTC (09:09 EDT), Israel Defense Forces (@IDF)
- **Source account:** Israel Defense Forces (@IDF)
- **Link:** https://x.com/IDF/status/2106371025667604860
- **Country:** Israel (Gaza)
- **Summary:** The IDF said it killed Ahmed Mahmoud Harb Ramlawi, described as a commander in the rocket array of the Shuja’iyya Battalion of Palestinian Islamic Jihad, and Munir Ibrahim Masr, described as head of an anti-tank missile cell in the same military wing. The IDF said both had worked to restore Islamic Jihad capabilities in violation of the ceasefire and were struck in the Gaza City area to remove that threat. A separate 2 October IDF post said a Hamas sniper, Tamer Hamaida, was killed in Khan Yunis for the same class of ceasefire-restoration activity. These are official military claims of targeted strikes; they are not reports of a new long-range missile transfer or a cross-border rocket barrage.
- **Estimated confidence:** Moderate (single official military account; no independent battle-damage confirmation in this scan)
